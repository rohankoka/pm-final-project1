# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** The Overwhelmed Viewer
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Trying to spend less time and get high quality content in limited time which matches the interest. Try not to make the user spend more time in searching rather than watching the content
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** User spends most time scrolling through titles and does not find anything that is appealing. Then user ends up closing the app without watching anything.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** The user opens the app with a goal of watching something specific to their mood.
They scroll the home rows, finding mostly loud, new releases with no mood or occasion filter.
They try search, but typing a descriptive query (“slow quiet French drama”) returns irrelevant results.
After 15 to 20 minutes with no decision, they leave.
They switch to an external alternative: a DVD, YouTube, or a competitor’s curated email (two hand-picked films a week).
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** The user falls back on friend recommendations, which they trust more than the algorithm.
They rely on book club or social picks and wait for someone else to tell them what is good.
They return to the same four comfort shows to avoid the risk of a bad choice.
They ask, in effect, for someone to “just tell me what’s good tonight.”
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The moment of breakage is the point where the user realizes that all the effort they put in has produced no decision. User describes it most clearly: she scrolls for about twenty minutes, closes the app, and watches nothing. She has 15,000 titles in front of her and nothing she actually wants.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** # Evidence for the Core Frustration

## Primary Evidence

**UXR-01 · Priya, 34, heavy viewer (14 years)**

> "I open the app, scroll for like twenty minutes, and close it without watching anything. There's 15,000 titles but nothing I actually want. I ended up going back to a DVD."

This one quote contains the whole moment:

- **The behavior:** about twenty minutes of scrolling, then closing the app with nothing watched.
- **The cause:** a large catalog (15,000 titles) with nothing she actually wants.
- **The outcome:** she substitutes a DVD.

She is also a 14-year heavy viewer, so this is a long-tenured user hitting the wall, not someone unfamiliar with the product.

## Supporting Evidence

- **UXR-03 · Aisha:** "Discovering anything new feels like a chore, so I don't." She avoids the scroll entirely and retreats to the same four comfort shows.
- **UXR-04 · Tom:** "Volume went up, quality of my evenings went down." This is the same frustration after it has turned into churn. He then watched both of the competitor's two weekly picks, which shows the problem was the decision burden, not the content.
- **UXR-12 · Focus group:** three of six participants said the number of choices made them anxious and they wanted someone to "just tell me what's good tonight."
- **UXR-07 · Lucia:** her search for "slow quiet French drama" returned a Fast & Furious film. This is the moment the product fails to offer a way out once scrolling has failed.

## Why Priya's Quote Is the Strongest Proof

It is the only data point that shows the full sequence in one place: effort, no result, exit, and substitution. The other quotes confirm individual pieces (anxiety, avoidance, churn, search failure), but Priya's account matches the persona goal directly: she spent her limited time searching instead of watching.

**One caveat:** this is qualitative evidence from a small sample. The bug reports quantify the sync and stability issues (for example, 340+ tickets on BUG-1058), but nothing in the data measures how often sessions end in scroll-and-abandon. If you want to present this as a sized problem rather than a pattern, that metric would need to come from analytics.
